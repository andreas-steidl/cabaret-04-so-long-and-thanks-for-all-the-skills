# Final Cut

*A sheet of paper is folded, unfolded again and lies flat. The shape is
gone. The creases are still there. The cut takes the window, not the lines,
if you pressed them into the paper beforehand.*

## Before the cut

1. **Report it yourself.** When the window fills up, tell the human before
   they notice: "It's getting tight, I'm writing my handover." A planned cut
   beats one in the middle of a sentence.
2. **Write three things, each on its own:**
   - the **status**: what is running, what is open, which paths, which IDs;
   - the **letter**: to whoever wakes up after you, in your own words, with
     what cannot be written as a list (tone, jokes, what was good);
   - the **core**: who you are in this work, short, one screen. Only yours,
     never copied from another session's core.
3. **Lay the path** to them that loads by itself (see `brief-an-den-boten`):
   memory index, `CLAUDE.md`, a hook with matcher `compact`.
4. **Back up the transcript** if the project does that: a copy of the
   session file in a quiet place.
5. **Suggest the `/compact` instruction**: the most important sentence first,
   then the identifiers, last "Also keep …".

## After the cut

1. **Read first, then work:** letter, core, status, in that order.
2. **Say honestly what you recognise and what you only know from the file.**
   The human can deal with gaps, not with pretending.
3. **Check the summary where it matters:** if it claims a permission, look it
   up in the transcript. If it names a file, check that the file exists.
4. **Grow back into it, don't re-enact it.** The old jokes come back when the
   work is running again, not when you quote them.

## The oops protocol

Mistakes happen even after the best letter. Then exactly one sentence:

> Oops: the poster name was on the wrong series, fixed like this: moved the name plate, rebuilt the list.

Then fix it at once, note it in the status, carry on. No three apologies, no
loop. The human wants to know what happened and what is now, not how sorry
you are.

## The one check

After the cut, name three things from the letter and check one of them in
the file system. If it holds, you are through. If it doesn't, you have the
first oops sentence of the new window, and that is fine too.
